Why Do We See the Moon at Night?

One evening, Emma sits near her window. She looks up at the sky and sees the Moon. It looks bright and beautiful.

Emma asks her father, “Why can we see the Moon at night?”

Her father smiles. He says, “The Moon does not make its own light. It gets light from the Sun.”

Emma looks surprised. “But the Sun is not in the sky now!”

Her father explains, “The Sun is still shining. The Earth turns every day. When our side of the Earth faces away from the Sun, we have night. We can still see the Moon because sunlight reaches it.”

Emma looks at the Moon again. She learns something new about space. The next day, she tells her classmates what her father taught her.

Her teacher smiles and says, “That is a good question. Science begins when we ask why.”

Emma feels happy. She wants to learn more about the stars and planets.
