The Old Clock

There is an old clock in the hall of a small hotel. It is tall and brown, and it has a round face with two long hands.

Every morning, the hotel owner, Mr. Lee, cleans the clock with a soft cloth. The clock belonged to his grandfather many years ago.

One day, a young guest named Peter looks at the clock.

“Excuse me,” he asks. “Why is this clock so special?”

Mr. Lee smiles. “My grandfather bought it when he opened this hotel. It has been here for more than fifty years.”

Peter looks at the clock again. “Does it still work?”

“Yes, but sometimes it is five minutes slow,” Mr. Lee says. “I need to remember to check it.”

Peter is interested in old things. His grandfather is a clock maker, and Peter often visits his workshop.

“Maybe I can help you,” Peter says.

The next morning, Peter brings a small tool from his grandfather's workshop. With Mr. Lee's permission, he helps check the clock.

They open the back and look at the small parts inside. Peter finds that one part needs cleaning. They clean it carefully and close the clock.

Soon, the clock begins to work better.

Mr. Lee is very happy. He thanks Peter and gives him a cup of hot chocolate.

Before Peter leaves the hotel, he takes a photo of the clock. He wants to show it to his grandfather.

Peter learns that old objects can tell stories about the past. With care and patience, people can keep them working for many more years.
