The Yellow River is the second-longest river in China. It is about 5,464 kilometers long and flows through nine provinces and regions. The river begins in the mountains of western China and flows east before reaching the Bohai Sea.

The Yellow River is often called the “Mother River of China.” It has played an important role in Chinese history and culture for thousands of years. Many early Chinese communities lived near the river because its water helped them grow food and raise animals.

The river gets its name from its yellow color. The water contains a large amount of yellow soil called loess. This soil comes from the land around the river. When the river carries the soil, its water becomes yellow and muddy.

However, the Yellow River has also caused serious problems. In the past, floods often destroyed villages, farmland, and homes. The river sometimes changed its course, making life difficult for people living nearby. Today, dams and other projects help control the water and reduce the danger of floods.

The Yellow River is also important for agriculture, industry, and electricity production. Millions of people depend on its water in their daily lives.

Protecting the Yellow River is a major environmental goal in China. People are planting trees, reducing pollution, and protecting the land around the river. These actions help keep the river healthy for future generations.

The Yellow River is more than a river. It is a symbol of Chinese civilization and the long history of the Chinese people.
