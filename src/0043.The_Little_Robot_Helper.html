Mr. Green is a science teacher. He likes making things with his students. This month, his class builds a small robot to help people at school.

The robot has two wheels, a camera, and a small screen. It can move around the classroom and carry light things.

One morning, Mr. Green puts a book on the floor.

“Can our robot find the book and bring it to my desk?” he asks.

The students are excited. They turn on the robot and give it a simple command.

First, the robot moves forward. Then it turns left and stops near the book. Its camera helps it see the book on the floor.

The robot moves closer and picks up the book with a small arm. Slowly, it carries the book to the teacher's desk.

“Great job!” the students say.

But the robot has one problem. It cannot move well on the thick carpet. Its wheels turn, but the robot stays in the same place.

The students look at the robot and think about the problem.

“Maybe we need bigger wheels,” says Lily.

Mr. Green smiles. “That is a good idea. When something does not work, we can test it and try again.”

The next day, the class changes the wheels and tests the robot again. This time, it moves across the carpet.

The students are proud of their work. They learn that building a robot takes time, patience, and many small improvements.
