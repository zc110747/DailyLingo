# The Rise of Ancient Rome

## Article

More than 2,500 years ago, a small city appeared near the Tiber River in Italy. This city was called Rome. According to an old story, two brothers named Romulus and Remus founded the city in 753 BC. Although this story may not be completely true, it became an important part of Roman history.

At first, Rome was only a small settlement. However, its location helped it grow. The city was built near a river, which provided water and made trade easier. It was also surrounded by hills, which offered some protection from enemies.

Over time, Rome became more powerful. Its people developed a strong army and began to control nearby areas. In 509 BC, the Romans removed their king and established a republic. This meant that citizens could choose some of their leaders. However, not everyone had the same political rights. Women and enslaved people could not vote.

The Roman Republic continued to expand. Roman soldiers traveled across Europe and around the Mediterranean Sea. They fought many wars and took control of large areas. As Rome became richer, problems also appeared. Some powerful leaders wanted to control the government.

In 27 BC, Augustus became the first Roman emperor. This marked the beginning of the Roman Empire. Under Roman rule, roads, bridges, public buildings, and water systems were built across the empire. These projects helped people travel, trade, and live in cities.

The Roman Empire eventually became too large and difficult to control. In AD 476, the Western Roman Empire officially came to an end. However, Roman ideas, laws, buildings, and language continued to influence many countries.

Today, when people visit Rome, they can still see ancient buildings and roads. These remains remind us that the Roman civilization played an important role in world history.

## Questions

**Question 1: According to the old story, who founded Rome?**

A. Augustus and Julius Caesar.

B. Romulus and Remus.

C. Two Roman soldiers.

**Question 2: Why was Rome's location helpful for its development?**

A. It was near a river and surrounded by hills.

B. It was close to the ocean and had many forests.

C. It had no nearby cities or enemies.

**Question 3: What happened in 509 BC?**

A. Rome became an empire.

B. Rome was destroyed by an enemy.

C. Rome established a republic.

**Question 4: Who could not vote in the Roman Republic?**

A. All Roman citizens.

B. Women and enslaved people.

C. Roman soldiers and leaders.

**Question 5: What happened in 27 BC?**

A. Augustus became the first Roman emperor.

B. Rome became a small settlement.

C. The Roman Republic expanded into Italy.

**Question 6: What did the Romans build across their empire?**

A. Airports and modern factories.

B. Schools and shopping centers only.

C. Roads, bridges, public buildings, and water systems.

**Question 7: Why did the Western Roman Empire come to an end?**

A. It became too large and difficult to control.

B. All its people moved to another country.

C. Its army stopped using roads.

**Question 8: What is the main idea of the article?**

A. Rome was only important because of its army.

B. Roman civilization had a lasting influence on the world.

C. Ancient people did not need roads or buildings.

## Answers

1. **B. Romulus and Remus.** According to an old Roman story, the two brothers founded Rome in 753 BC.

2. **A. It was near a river and surrounded by hills.** The river supported trade and provided water, while the hills offered protection.

3. **C. Rome established a republic.** The Romans removed their king and created a new political system.

4. **B. Women and enslaved people.** They did not have the same political rights as male citizens.

5. **A. Augustus became the first Roman emperor.** His rule marked the beginning of the Roman Empire.

6. **C. Roads, bridges, public buildings, and water systems.** These projects improved transportation, trade, and daily life.

7. **A. It became too large and difficult to control.** Managing such a large empire became increasingly difficult.

8. **B. Roman civilization had a lasting influence on the world.** Roman laws, buildings, ideas, and language influenced many later societies.
