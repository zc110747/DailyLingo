# The American Civil War

## Article

The American Civil War was one of the most important events in American history. It lasted from 1861 to 1865 and was fought between the Northern and Southern states. The war changed the future of the United States and brought an end to slavery across the country.

During the 19th century, the North and South developed in different ways. The Northern states had many factories, businesses, and growing cities. Their economy depended mainly on industry and trade. In contrast, the Southern states depended heavily on agriculture. Large farms produced cotton, tobacco, and other crops. Many of these farms used enslaved African Americans as workers.

Slavery became the biggest source of conflict between the two regions. Many people in the North wanted to stop the spread of slavery, while Southern leaders wanted to protect it. The two sides also disagreed about the power of the federal government and the rights of individual states.

In 1860, Abraham Lincoln was elected president. He opposed the expansion of slavery into new territories. Several Southern states decided to leave the United States and formed a new government called the Confederate States of America. In April 1861, fighting began when Confederate forces attacked Fort Sumter in South Carolina.

Lincoln led the Northern states, known as the Union. The Southern states were led by Jefferson Davis. Both sides prepared large armies, and the war continued for four difficult years. Thousands of soldiers died in major battles, and many families lost their homes and loved ones.

In January 1863, Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation. It declared freedom for enslaved people in areas that were still fighting against the Union. This changed the purpose of the war and allowed African American men to join the Union Army.

In April 1865, the Confederate army surrendered, and the war came to an end. Later that year, the Thirteenth Amendment to the Constitution officially abolished slavery throughout the United States.

However, the end of slavery did not immediately bring equal rights to everyone. African Americans continued to face discrimination for many years.

The Civil War showed how serious disagreements could divide a country. It also changed American society, government, and history forever.

A. From 1775 to 1783.

B. From 1861 to 1865.

A. Lincoln gave his famous speech.

**Question 7: What did the Emancipation Proclamation do?**

A. It made every state independent.

B. It declared freedom for enslaved people in areas still rebelling against the Union.

C. It ended the war immediately.

**Question 8: What did the Thirteenth Amendment accomplish?**

A. It officially abolished slavery throughout the United States.

B. It created the American army.

C. It gave every state the right to leave the Union.

## Answers

1. **B. From 1861 to 1865.** The American Civil War lasted four years and ended in 1865.

2. **A. The North depended more on industry, while the South depended more on agriculture.** Factories and businesses were important in the North, while farming played a major role in the Southern economy.

3. **C. Slavery.** The disagreement over slavery was the central issue that divided the two regions.

4. **B. Abraham Lincoln.** Lincoln won the presidential election in 1860 and became the 16th president of the United States.

5. **A. They disagreed with the federal government, especially over slavery.** Several Southern states wanted to protect slavery and establish an independent government.

6. **C. Confederate forces attacked Fort Sumter.** The attack took place in South Carolina on April 12, 1861, and marked the beginning of the Civil War.

7. **B. It declared freedom for enslaved people in areas still rebelling against the Union.** Lincoln issued the proclamation on January 1, 1863. It did not immediately end slavery everywhere.

8. **A. It officially abolished slavery throughout the United States.** The Thirteenth Amendment was ratified in December 1865 and made slavery illegal across the country.
